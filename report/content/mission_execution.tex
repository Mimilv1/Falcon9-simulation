\section{Mission Execution and Numerical Implementation}
\label{sec:mission-exec}

The mission layer connects the models of Chapters~1--7. Commands are A-class project designs: official schedule entries anchor selected events and state-triggered conditions determine return transitions. This chapter defines execution; observed times belong in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Ascent and upper-stage commands}
The ascent program tilts the nose away from local vertical. Equation~\eqref{eq:ascent-command} defines its target angle and ideal angular-acceleration command, with $x=\max[(t-8\,\mathrm{s})/(100\,\mathrm{s}),0]$ and Body attitude error $\vect e_R$:
\begin{equation}
 \theta_{\mathrm{cmd}}=80^\circ\frac{x^2}{1+x^2},\qquad
 \vect\alpha_{\mathrm{cmd}}=k_p\vect e_R-k_d\vect\omega,\qquad
 k_p=2\,\mathrm{s^{-2}},\quad k_d=2.5\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}.
\label{eq:ascent-command}
\end{equation}
Here $\vect e_R=\mat C_{BE}^{\mathsf T}(\vect n\times\vect n_{\mathrm{cmd}})$, with actual and commanded unit nose directions in $E$. The implemented direction uses projected launch-east. Although the configuration contains a $45^\circ$ heading field, the current controller does not use that field in constructing the target; no reconstructed ISS ascent plane is claimed. The pitch timescale and operating point were calibrated against the ascent record used again in Section~\ref{sec:ascent-results}.

All nine engines initially run at full throttle. The shutdown envelope of Equation~\eqref{eq:shutdown} reduces both thrust and flow from 140 to $141\,\mathrm{s}$; zero-flow guards then permit separation at $144\,\mathrm{s}$. The conserved upper child ignites at 152 and cuts off at $545\,\mathrm{s}$: throttle 0.95, one-second ramps, $200\,\mathrm{km}$ height target and a $0.05\,\mathrm{s}$ maximum step. Height/radial-speed feedback selects attitude. Its drag uses $C_D=0.3$ on the stage cross-section, not the declared \texttt{second\_stack} configuration.

\subsection{Return commands and fuel management}
Table~\ref{tab:return-settings} collects the principal return settings. The three-engine set is the central engine and an opposite outer pair (indices 0, 1, 5); landing uses only the central engine. Restart guards require separation, zero flow, engines off, available fuel and assumed propulsion readiness, plus the required descent condition and pointing error below $25^\circ$. Propellant settling and ignition health are assumed rather than simulated.

Boostback points against the reduced-order predicted impact miss. The predictor advances central gravity, frame acceleration, approximate drag and a prospective entry burn in $0.5\,\mathrm{s}$ steps; it is refreshed once per second. Steering opposes the predicted horizontal miss with a time-to-impact gain and an upward bias, then normalizes the result to a direction. The first satisfied miss, fuel or duration condition starts shutdown; a 500 kg margin anticipates ramp consumption.

Entry starts on descending altitude and ends on speed or fuel cap. A constrained force solver selects pointing from desired vertical/lateral acceleration and aerodynamic reaction. Landing starts when the $0.1\,\mathrm{s}$ stopping predictor leaves no more than the declared clearance allowance.

Landing feedback operates on the lowest leg tip. If $h_\ell$ and $\dot h_\ell$ are its clearance and radial velocity, the commanded contact acceleration is $[-\dot h_\ell|\dot h_\ell|-v_t^2]/[2\max(h_\ell,0.005\,\mathrm{m})]$, with target $v_t$ from Table~\ref{tab:return-settings}. Rotational, moving-CG and ground-curvature corrections convert this to the required CG acceleration. Lateral position and velocity feedback drive the assumed target. Unused boostback/entry allocations may transfer to landing, while the reserve and limiting-tank residual are protected; a 200 kg margin anticipates landing shutdown. Contact geometry and acceptance limits are defined in Section~\ref{sec:vehicle}.

\subsection{Force/moment allocation and actuator representation}
The force solver respects the tilt envelope. Throttle ramps are numerical envelopes, not additional validated steady operating points. Fins receive static deflections through Section~\ref{sec:aero}; travel-rate dynamics are absent.

Equation~\eqref{eq:ideal-moment} distinguishes the ideal attitude input from physical TVC:
\begin{equation}
 \vect M_{\mathrm{ideal}}=\mat J_C\vect\alpha_{\mathrm{cmd}},\qquad
 \mat J_C^{-1}\vect M_{\mathrm{ideal}}=\vect\alpha_{\mathrm{cmd}}.
\label{eq:ideal-moment}
\end{equation}
The ascent couple is unlimited. Return control adds gyroscopic feedforward and aerodynamic-moment compensation, allocates fin demand, then saturates the residual ideal couple at the declared authority. Changing-inertia and exhaust-flow terms still enter the physical dynamics. Inertia cancellation in the commanded contribution is model inversion, not a loss of inertia or proof that aerodynamic stability removes the need for TVC. \path{Actuators.py} remains empty and the propulsion evaluator rejects nonzero gimbal angles. Thus landing results test an assumed moment authority, not nozzle/servo dynamics or closed-loop robustness to actuator/model error.

\subsection{Integration, event and sensor timing}
The accepted-state loop follows this order:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Evaluate phase conditions and accept a due event; apply engine/configuration changes and preserve both children at separation.
\item Generate due sensor samples and update navigation. Record truth/estimate output when due.
\item Select a step bounded by $0.01\,\mathrm{s}$, scheduled events, control/ramp knots, output time and the next sensor clock. Near contact, clearance and speed impose a tighter bound.
\item Apply RK4 to the physical state with trial-state loads and mass properties, then synchronize the accepted vehicle object.
\end{enumerate}
RHS evaluations are pure with respect to clocks and random sampling. Guidance normally updates every $0.2\,\mathrm{s}$; landing adapts that interval between 0.02 and $0.2\,\mathrm{s}$. Sensors retain their independent clocks from Table~\ref{tab:sensors}, and output is normally every $0.1\,\mathrm{s}$. The internal translation representation follows Section~\ref{sec:vehicle}.

When a trial crosses the ground sphere at a leg tip, bisection refines the contact epoch with up to 35 iterations and a $10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s}$ interval criterion. Acceptance uses the pre-contact state; position, speed or attitude are not reset to create success. Propellant-limit, model-domain and unmet-event failures produce explicit run status. A full run requires both an acceptable contact and completion of the intended return sequence.

\subsection{Two-vehicle execution and reproducible operation}
The booster is advanced until contact or failure. The saved upper-child state is then propagated on its own time interval, using the same mission clock; the two trajectories are physically independent after separation even though their computations are executed sequentially. Navigation remains attached to stage 1.

From the repository root, install the pinned dependencies and execute:
\begin{quote}\ttfamily\small
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt\\
python main.py --case full --no-show
\end{quote}
The full case requests 600 s; booster output ends at contact and the upper child continues. \texttt{--output} selects a fresh directory; \texttt{--plot-only} reopens saved results without integration.

Outputs are truth for both vehicles, measurements, navigation, events/timeline, ascent comparison data and \path{summary.json}. The summary retains settings, seed, status and input hashes. Section~\ref{sec:results} identifies the records behind its figures and tables; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-reproduction} gives explicit run settings, file contracts and audit fingerprints.
