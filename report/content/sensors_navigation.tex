\section{Sensors and Navigation}
\label{sec:navigation}

The navigation subsystem generates sensor measurements from the truth
trajectory and estimates the vehicle state with a nine-state Kalman
filter. Sampling occurs only at accepted integration times: no random
numbers are drawn during integration trial evaluations, so a fixed seed
reproduces a run exactly.

Table~\ref{tab:sensors} summarizes the three models. The \ac{IMU}
measures specific force and angular rate at its fixed structural
station; the measurement model includes the station kinematics
(angular-rate and center-of-gravity migration terms) of the dynamics
core. The \ac{GNSS} receiver provides position and velocity in the
external frame, with a Gauss-Markov correlated error beside the white
noise, lever-arm correction for its antenna station, outage and dropout
support, and a latency model that carries and interpolates the delayed
samples. The \ac{LIDAR} measures the slant range to the spherical ground
along its boresight; its update uses the analytic intersection geometry
with the planet sphere and rejects invalid pointing geometry rather
than extrapolating.

\subsection{Nine-state navigation filter}

The filter (\texttt{kalman.py}) estimates \ac{IMU}-station position,
velocity and accelerometer bias in the external frame,
$\mathbf{x} = [\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{b}_a]$, with the
strapdown prediction
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nav-predict}
\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{R}\,(\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{meas}} - \hat{\mathbf{b}}_a)
+ \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{p}) + \mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{fr}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{v}),
\quad
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{p}^{+} &= \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{v}\,\Delta t
+ \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{a}\,\Delta t^2, \\
\mathbf{v}^{+} &= \mathbf{v} + \mathbf{a}\,\Delta t,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
%
where the model gravity and rotating-frame acceleration come from the
environment model of Section~\ref{sec:environment}, linearized through
the same gravity and frame-gradient maps that the environment module
supplies. The attitude rotation $\mathbf{R}$ is the truth attitude: the
filter does not estimate attitude or gyro bias --- a declared, explicit
limitation of the current implementation, not an oversight. The process
noise follows from the accelerometer power spectral density
$\sigma_a^2 / f_{\mathrm{IMU}}$ propagated through the strapdown
kinematics, plus a bias random-walk term; the initial covariance is
$\mathrm{diag}([2,2,2,\,0.05,0.05,0.05,\,0.1,0.1,0.1])$ with a seeded
initial position error of $[1, -1, 0.5]\,\mathrm{m}$.

GNSS updates correct position and velocity with the antenna lever arm
and rotate the arm with the angular rate; latency is compensated by
extrapolating the measurement with the current acceleration estimate.
LiDAR updates are scalar and nonlinear, with the measurement Jacobian
taken analytically at the sphere intersection. Both updates use the
Joseph-form covariance update, and normalized innovation squares are
recorded for monitoring. One filter instance and the same first-stage
sensor stations run across separation without a reset to the new true
center of gravity --- the estimated state is the IMU-station state, and
that convention is retained deliberately through the staging event.

The sensor and filter chain is verified end-to-end by the cross-module
and mission-level test suites; in the nominal full-mission run the
navigation position error holds $0.918\,\mathrm{m}$ \ac{RMS} with truth
attitude (quoted and discussed in the results chapter of this report).
